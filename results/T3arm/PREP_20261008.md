**V1: what the export changes, and how to compare.** Thanks to the patch, `max_len`·`head_max_len`·`model_name` keep their base values. The following change:
- `temperature` refit: english [1.637, 1.251, 1.983] → [1.475, 1.2, 1.2] (T2 calib 400, only choice fitted, the others 1.2 because they have 0 samples). multilingual [1, 1, 1] → [2.094, 1.2, 1.2] (T7 calib).

- `fine_tuned: true`, `max_tokens_per_batch: 2048` added (not read by inference).

temperature does not change the argmax of a question. So **the accuracy and McNemar comparisons remain valid as they are**. On the other hand, **ECE, Brier, NLL and coverage mix in the refit effect.** The refit alone changed ECE from 0.083 → 0.064 on english T2 Jev and from 0.365 → 0.179 on multilingual T7-ko Jev. To see the training effect alone, a 0-step export like V1 (vanilla weights + T fitted on that task's calib) should be set up per task as a "vanilla + T" baseline, and trained's calibration metrics compared with it (docs/11 open question 4; about 15-25 s of calibration forward per task + evaluation). Calibration tables also state the temperature source (upstream fit / 1.0 / task calib fit).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- docs/11 criterion ① permuted ≤ majority + 0.05 = 0.346 is **not met** (0.385; 0.475 at 2 epochs). ② real labels − permuted ≥ 0.30 is **passed** (0.446).
- The label path works. On the same items, real labels give +0.219 over vanilla and permuted labels −0.227 relative to vanilla. The train CE of permuted training stays at ln 6 ≈ 1.79 and its train acc is at chance (0.15-0.21). So in 1 epoch it did not memorize the permuted labels. By the end of 2 epochs train acc rises to 0.22-0.25, and it starts to memorize some of them.
- The part above majority is read as residual pretraining ranking, not leakage. Permuted labels are unrelated to the true labels, so leakage would, if anything, lower dev. The permuted model's correct answers concentrate on items vanilla got right (0.475 vs 0.242). Predictions collapse onto joy, the mode of the training label distribution (85%), and confidence is nearly uniform (0.183). The remaining correct answers come from sadness (recall 0.31) and anger (0.10), where vanilla is strong. At 2 epochs, as the joy collapse weakens, the vanilla ranking shows again (0.675 vs 0.160).
- Criterion ① assumes that training collapses the base to chance. It does not fit a base that is strong zero-shot (0.612 on this dev). An alternative criterion is "permuted < vanilla zero-shot, and real − permuted ≥ 0.30", and both parts pass. The verdict is an owner decision (Next actions).

## Anomalies / notes

**Warning messages.** Every load of the english·typed-decisions checkpoints prints `laya: this checkpoint ships invalid temperatures or values outside [0.5, 5]; using choice:11+=0.1006 -> 0.5` (46 times). This bucket is used only for choice questions with 11 or more options, and our sets have at most 10 options, so there is no effect. In the decode check of `ft_task.py items`, transformers prints `Ignoring clean_up_tokenization_spaces=True for BPE tokenizer` 2 times. It only concerns decode, and for exact option-string comparison it is in fact the right behavior. It is unrelated to the encoding of training items. Otherwise there is no fallback, no CPU substitution and no NaN.

**Differences from the plan (docs/11, the brief) and points of interpretation.**
1. **T8 hold-out.** prompt-injections has no validation split. dev 60 + calib 40 are split off by stratification from the 546 train rows (pool 544), and all of the remaining 444 rows are trained on for 3 epochs. "use all of it" was read as not applying the 5,000 cap, which matches the docs/11 §3 table ("everything except dev·calib"). calib 40 is within the cap min(400, N//10) = 48. dev 60 is coarse for epoch selection.
2. **All train samples are label-stratified.** The evaluation sets are stratified, so the priors were matched. In T10 train, the quota shortfall of HR (274) and General Inquiry (202) is filled from the other queues. Only T8 train has the natural distribution (False 293 / True 151).
3. **The `pool_of` filter is also applied to train/dev/calib.** 2-2,000 characters per state field, the same rule as for the evaluation sets. In T9 train, 6,145 rows (19%, mostly body > 2,000 characters) are dropped, and in T10 2,588 rows with an empty subject. This differs slightly from upstream's training and evaluation distributions. Our train and evaluation match each other.
4. **The T9·T10 Jev-format sets and ext exclude text that overlaps train.** 261 rows (16%) of the T9 test equal train after normalization. Therefore 0 rows have to be removed from training. The frozen T1-T8 sets keep their original rule, and the overlapping train rows are removed from training instead (T3 1, T7 24).
5. **T7 ext selection.** Among the ids present in the test of all 5 languages, ids whose utterance overlaps the train of that language in any language (486) were removed. Then 100 ids were chosen, stratified over the 59 intents. Options are drawn from a single `Random(13)` stream per id and shared by the 5 languages. T7 train is an independent sample per language (1,000 per language), and its ids were not matched.
6. **T7 `cooking_query`.** It is a train-only label, so 4 rows per language were removed from the train candidates, because it is outside the 59 evaluation labels.
7. **JSONL escaping.** The new JSONL files write U+0085/U+2028/U+2029 as `\u` escapes (the same JSON values). This prevents `scripts/metrics_extra.py read_dataset` (str.splitlines) from splitting rows at the NEL characters in Enron bodies. laya's reader was never affected. The affected files are only T9 train/dev/calib.
8. **V1 method.** `--epochs 0` is the closest 0-step method. It goes through the calibration forward and export as usual, with no optimizer step. At the end the script also writes the config to `checkpoint_latest/`, but that directory is only created when there is an epoch. So the runner (`run_ft.py --zero-step`) creates the empty directory first.
9. **The real-label reference for V3.** docs/11 says to compare with the epoch 1 dev of the main-run T3. Since the main run had not happened yet, a run with the same 5,000 items × 1 epoch (cosine ending at 1 epoch) was used as the control. After the main run, the verdict is redone with the epoch 1 dev in T3's `diagnostics.json`.
10. **Epoch selection.** As in docs/11 §4, the epoch with the highest dev accuracy is chosen, the earlier one on ties (the brief's "report both" is satisfied by keeping the dev of every epoch in `select.json` and `diagnostics.json`). For a non-final epoch, `ft_task.py calibrate` fits the temperature on the same calib rows. The difference is that it computes logits from the saved fp16 weights, while the script's own export fits with the fp32 weights in memory. The final export and the last-epoch copy have the same weight sha256 (confirmed in V5).
11. **Driver diagnostics extended to match docs/11 §4.** Added epoch 0 (= vanilla) dev, per-class recall, a temperature clamp flag, and a "training failure" flag (the dev of every epoch below vanilla). 12. **Frozen files.** Rerunning the T1-T8 builders in scratch changes the headers of T4·T7·T1_noulnocrit. This comes from the 2026-10-07 relabeling of the training mix and is expected (the rows are identical). This work did not write those files.
13. **Upstream budget statement.** The "\~30k questions × 4 epochs" in README.md:1532 does not match the pinned dataset revision (train 1,200 cases = 6,000 decisions). Computed from the Kaggle notebook settings it is 5,600 × 4 = 22,400 item-passes (effective batch 64, 350 steps, fp16, max_len 1024).
14. **T10 base.** The Router (the rule for choosing the base) sends T10 to english. But vanilla multilingual is much better on ext, 0.454 vs 0.264. The decision is followed as is, and the other base is kept as a reference column in the tables.

## Found during the main training run: spread between T1 retrainings with the same settings (2026-10-08 11:55)

The main-run T1 has the same script sha256, items sha256, seed 13 and all 15 script arguments as V2(b) (`results/T3arm/T1/train_timing.json`, `validity/V2_T1_timing.json`). The results differ.
